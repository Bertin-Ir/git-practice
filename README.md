# State of the Software Engineering Job Market in 2026

**Article:** [State of the software engineering job market in 2026](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/state-of-the-job-market-2026)
**Source:** The Pragmatic Engineer (Gergely Orosz)

## Why it's interesting

Most writing about software engineering and AI right now is speculation. This piece is different because it argues from hiring data. Orosz tracks job postings across countries and companies and shows that the picture is more uneven than either the "AI is killing dev jobs" or the "everything is fine" camps suggest.

A few findings stand out. Software engineering listings are growing again in the US and UK, but they are still below their 2021 peak, and Germany and France are moving the other way. Inside Big Tech the growth is mixed: Apple and Google are adding engineers while Microsoft and Amazon are slightly down. Meanwhile demand is shifting toward AI engineering and forward-deployed roles, which now out-pay traditional software engineering, while mobile and frontend demand is cooling.

The part that matters most for anyone early in their career is that new grads and interns are having a harder time getting hired, even as top companies expand. That makes the article less a headline about the industry and more a map of where the openings actually are, and which skills (ML systems, deployment, working directly with customers) are pulling ahead.

## Worth reading if you

- Are a student or new grad planning your first roles
- Want data, not vibes, on how AI is changing engineering hiring
- Are deciding between a general SWE path and an AI/ML engineering path

## Comments ~ Sam Evans

This article is useful because it moves beyond hype and looks at actual hiring data. The key takeaway is that the market is not collapsing, but it is becoming more specialized: AI, deployment, and customer-facing engineering roles are growing, while some traditional areas are cooling. For early-career engineers, that means focusing on high-demand skills and practical experience is more important than ever.
